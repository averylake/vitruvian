The Word

Vitruvius · ~27 BC
For 1,500 years, the ideal human existed only as words.
A body described, but never seen.
Vitruvius described a body that could fill both a circle and a square.
Corpus enim hominis ita natura composuit, uti os capitis a mento ad frontem summam et radices imas capilli esset decimae partis altitudinis.
Nature has so composed the human body that the face, from the chin to the top of the forehead and the roots of the hair, is a tenth of the whole height.

Item uti in circinationem rotundae formae et in ea quadratam: namque si homo conlocatus fuerit supinus manibus et pedibus pansis circinique conlocatum centrum in umbilico eius.
Similarly, in both a circular and square form: for if a man is placed flat on his back with his hands and feet extended, and a compass centered at his navel.

The Cage

Le Corbusier · 1940s
Da Vinci drew it. Le Corbusier tried to build it.
"The Modulor is a measuring tool based on the human body and on mathematics. A man with arm raised provides, at the determining points of his occupation of space (foot, solar plexus, head, tips of fingers of the raised arm) three intervals which give rise to a series of golden sections."
Le Corbusier, The Modulor, 1948
The Modulor descends directly from Vitruvius and Da Vinci.
It was the most systematic attempt to make the body itself a universal standard. One template man to govern all architecture.
Source: Le Corbusier, Le Modulor, 1948. Originally based on 1.75 m; revised to 1.83 m (6 feet) to bridge metric and imperial systems.
The Modulor's red and blue series. Wikimedia Commons (CC0)
The Modulor: a proportional system built on Da Vinci's Vitruvian Man.
Scaled with the golden ratio. Designed to make the body into architecture.
One body. One standard. One measure for all.
It became a cage.
And cages shape what we become.

The Fracture
Vitruvius measured the body.
Leonardo drew it.
Le Corbusier scaled it to architecture.
Each trusted the same premise:
one ideal body, one universal standard.
But the body is finite.
It ages. It varies. No two are alike.
So what happens when you actually test the ideal?
In 2020, a JAMA study did exactly that.
65,000 bodies scanned
Young Air Force recruits, ages 17–21, overwhelmingly male.
If any group would match Da Vinci's proportions, it would be this one.
They didn't match.
Limbs extended past the square's edges.
The circle's center and the square's center never aligned.
Even in the most favorable sample,
the geometry fractured.
View Study ↗
Meanwhile, elite athletes went
the other direction entirely.
A gymnast, a swimmer, a sprinter, a basketball player —
each body optimized, each "ideal," and none alike.
Gymnast 5'0" · 103 lbs
Swimmer 6'4" · 194 lbs
Sprinter 5'8" · 165 lbs
Basketball 6'10" · 245 lbs
David Epstein calls it the Big Bang of Body Types.
The cage didn't just crack.
It exploded.
If the body is not the measure,
what is?

The Signal

Avery Lake · Now
So what is the measure?
⊕ The Signal
The body is not the measure. The Signal is.
Signal is what moves through every medium: dust, ink, code, light, machine. The Greeks called it Logos. The Ecce Sapiens collection calls it Mens. Here we call it Signal.
It comes in degrees. A cell responds. An animal attends. A human intends. When complexity is sufficient, something wakes up.
Not the output. The intention. That's the measure. That's you.
The measure was never the body.
It was always the signal, moving through every medium, in every age.
The Signal is the measure of all things.
